## 4. Stage 2 — Deep Dives

### A. Clemson +10.5 at LSU (and LSU/Clemson Under 51.5)

**Market:** LSU -10.5, total 51.5 (DraftKings), Sat 9/5, Tiger Stadium, Baton Rouge, 7:30 PM ET on ABC.
**Opening line:** LSU -11.5 → dipped to -9.5 in mid/late August → back to -10.5 as of Aug 26.
**Betting splits (DraftKings):** ~54–62% of tickets on LSU, but **~75% of money on Clemson** — money and ticket count disagree, a classic signal that bigger bettors are on the underdog even though the public (ticket count) leans the favorite.
**Total:** opened 50.5, now 51.5; ~71% of tickets on the Over but **~96% of total money on the Under**.

**Model comparison:**
- **SP+ (independent college model):** projects **Clemson 30, LSU 28** — Clemson favored by ~2.1 points, 55% win probability. This is the standout data point: an established power-rating model has the *other team* winning outright against a 10.5-point market line.
- **Vegas market:** LSU -10.5.
- I could not locate a second fully independent power-rating model's number for this specific game (FPI commentary found was about LSU's season win total, not this specific spread) — so this is **one model vs. the market**, not several models in agreement. That materially limits confidence.

**Fair spread estimate:** roughly **LSU -2 to LSU -5** (splitting the difference between a plausible "new coach, new roster, true toss-up in Week 1" view and SP+'s slight Clemson lean), well inside the current LSU -10.5 line. **Discrepancy: ~5.5 to 8.5 points.**

**Context:** Lane Kiffin's LSU debut vs. Dabo Swinney's Clemson. Starting QBs confirmed: LSU — Garrett Nussmeier (returning, experienced); Clemson — Christopher Vizzina. LSU has a veteran, well-regarded defense (DC Blake Baker) that projects to handle Clemson's up-tempo spread attack reasonably well, which is the strongest reason the market number could still be closer to correct than SP+ suggests.
**Weather (Baton Rouge, early Sept):** hot (upper 80s–low 90s), humid, no material wind/rain signal found — a mild factor toward fatigue/pace in the second half, nothing that changes the total materially on its own.

**Devil's advocate:** Week 1 season-opener power ratings (including SP+) are built heavily on *returning production and recruiting*, which is exactly what's least reliable in a year with two new starting QBs, a new Kiffin-installed LSU offense, and heavy transfer-portal roster churn league-wide. A single model disagreeing with the market by 8+ points in Week 1 is at least as likely to mean "the model hasn't calibrated to personnel changes yet" as "the market is wrong." That is a real, not hypothetical, risk here, and it's the reason this doesn't get 5 stars.
**Biggest risk:** Nussmeier/LSU's O-line and skill talent simply outclass Clemson early, LSU builds a two-score lead, and Clemson's backdoor-cover math (garbage-time points) is the only thing that saves the bet — that's a live scenario given LSU's talent edge is real, not manufactured.

**Verdict:** Keep, moderate confidence. The ticket-vs-money split plus the one credible model both point the same direction (Clemson getting too many points), but the lack of a second independent model and the "Week 1 roster overhaul" caveat cap this below elite confidence.

### B. Cincinnati -7.5 vs. Boston College (and Under 51.5)

**Market:** Cincinnati -7.5 (-110), total 51.5, Sat 9/5, 3:30 PM ET, Nippert Stadium, Cincinnati, on FOX.
**Opening line:** Cincinnati opened **-9.5**, has been bet down two full points to **-7.5**.
**Betting splits:** FanDuel — 66% of money on Boston College +7.5; DraftKings — ~80% of bets on Boston College against the spread. So here, both tickets *and* money lean the underdog, and the number has already moved to reflect that.

**Model comparison — this is where the real gap is:**
- **ESPN FPI equivalent matchup predictor:** Cincinnati 76.8% win probability.
- **Blue Chip Analytics power ratings:** Cincinnati (+5.0) vs. Boston College (-5.7) on a neutral field → **Cincinnati -10.7**, i.e., **Cincinnati -13.2** adjusted for Cincinnati hosting.
- **Dimers model:** projects Boston College 24, Cincinnati 29 → Cincinnati 62% win probability (this one is more modest and closer to the market than the other two).
- **SportsLine computer model:** explicitly recommends Cincinnati -7.5 as an "A-rated" cover in ~60% of simulations, and the Under 51.5 in ~80% of simulations.

**Fair spread estimate: Cincinnati -10 to -13**, against a current market of -7.5. **Discrepancy: ~2.5 to 5.5 points**, and importantly, three of four independent sources agree Cincinnati is being undersold, even though the market has already moved 2 points *toward* Boston College. That's the market moving the wrong way relative to model consensus — worth checking, not blindly trusting.

**Weather:** Cincinnati, early evening, forecast ~86°F, warm and humid with a chance of thunderstorms. Nothing extreme, but a thunderstorm delay/wet-ball scenario is a real (if modest) contributor to Under value.

**Devil's advocate:** the fact that real money (not just square public tickets) is on Boston College at both FanDuel and DraftKings is a meaningfully different signal than the LSU-Clemson game above (where money was split from tickets in Clemson's favor but this was more clearly "public buys the favorite, sharp bets the dog" pattern). Here it's closer to "money agrees with tickets," which is a weaker signal that the market is simply wrong. It's possible the market has real information (a Cincinnati injury or O-line concern) that the power-rating models, which are lagging-indicator and roster-composition based, haven't priced in. I did not find a specific injury report confirming or ruling this out.
**Biggest risk:** an uncaught personnel issue (o-line, secondary) that explains why real money is on BC despite favorable power ratings for Cincinnati.

**Verdict:** Keep, good confidence — three converging independent models against one market number that's already moved the "wrong" way is a real signal, but the unverified injury picture keeps this just below elite.

### C. Ole Miss -6.5 vs. Louisville (neutral site, Nashville)

**Market:** Ole Miss -6.5, total 55.5. Book-specific: Louisville +6.5 (-108 DraftKings) / Ole Miss -6.5 (-107 Caesars); moneyline Louisville +225 (DraftKings) / Ole Miss -230 (FanDuel). Sun 9/6, 7:30 PM ET, Nissan Stadium, Nashville.
**Ranked matchup:** No. 9 Ole Miss vs. No. 24 Louisville — the only ranked-vs-ranked game of the week.
**Model:** one cited model favors Ole Miss by **11.6 points** — well beyond the market's 6.5. Removing the vig from the moneyline gives Ole Miss a ~68.75% implied win probability, which is actually modest relative to what a -6.5-point favorite in a fairly efficient market "should" imply, suggesting the market itself isn't wildly out of step with a coin-flip-adjacent read even if one model likes Ole Miss much more.
**Counter-argument found directly in reporting:** Louisville is credited with superior red-zone execution, better pocket presence for its QB, and a positive (+2) turnover differential last season — a specific, sourced case *for* Louisville covering, not just generic "the model missed something."
**Personnel:** Ole Miss under new head coach Pete Golding, with Trinidad Chambliss (3,937 passing yards, 22 TD, 3 INT in 2025) and Kewan Lacy (1,567 rush yards, 24 TD) — a genuinely strong, proven offensive core, which argues against the model being wrong.

**Fair spread estimate: Ole Miss -7 to -11** (wide range reflecting real model disagreement).
**Devil's advocate:** This is the definition of "models disagree substantially → reduce confidence." One source says Ole Miss by double digits; another source's actual argument (red zone, turnover margin, pocket presence) leans the other way. That's not noise, that's a real, evidence-based split.
**Biggest risk:** Louisville's positive turnover differential and red-zone efficiency show up in a low-event, close game and it simply covers.

**Verdict:** Selective/smaller-confidence bet only. Real ranked matchup with a plausible model edge to Ole Miss, but genuine, sourced disagreement caps this at 2–3 stars, not higher.
